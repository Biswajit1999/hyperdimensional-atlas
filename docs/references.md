# References and further reading

These are genuine, standard sources. No page numbers, DOIs, direct quotations, or URLs
are invented; where a specific edition is not stated, treat the entry as a pointer to
the work rather than a precise citation. Readers should consult the works directly for
exact details.

## Higher-dimensional geometry

- **H. S. M. Coxeter — *Regular Polytopes*.** The classic reference on polytopes in
  arbitrary dimension, including the hypercube and its symmetry and face structure.
- **Thomas F. Banchoff — educational work on higher-dimensional geometry.** Banchoff
  produced influential teaching material and visualisations of four-dimensional
  geometry; his writing is a good entry point to seeing higher dimensions through
  projection.
- **Edwin A. Abbott — *Flatland: A Romance of Many Dimensions*.** A 19th-century work
  of **fiction**, included only as a historical analogy for reasoning about dimensions
  we cannot directly perceive. It is a story, not a scientific source.

## Physics

- **A reputable general relativity textbook** — for example, a standard graduate text
  on general relativity covering the curved four-dimensional spacetime manifold and the
  Lorentzian signature. (Consult a current edition from a major academic publisher.)
- **A reputable introductory string theory textbook** — for the role of extra
  dimensions and compactification in string/M-theory, presented as theoretical
  framework rather than confirmed observation.

## Tools

- **Three.js documentation** — the official documentation for the WebGL library used to
  render the scene (`https://threejs.org/docs/`).

## A note on honesty

This list deliberately avoids fabricated specifics. If you need an exact citation for
a claim, go to the primary source and record the edition, chapter, and page yourself.
The atlas separates what is mathematically certain from what is physically speculative,
and the reading list is meant to support that separation rather than blur it.
